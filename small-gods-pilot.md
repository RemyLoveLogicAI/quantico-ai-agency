# SMALL GODS (adult animated series, 8 x 22 min)

**Logline:** Dolores "Dee" Okafor-Reyes, a burnt-out property manager, inherits a rent-controlled Brooklyn walk-up where every unit houses a minor deity the modern world has stopped needing. She has 30 days to keep the building solvent, and the gods have never once paid rent.

**Tone:** Rick and Morty's cosmic mess, Bojack's ache, Letterkenny's rhythm. Funny first, then it quietly wrecks you.

## The tenants
- **1A, Gary, God of Parking Tickets.** Cheerful middle-manager energy. Was feared in 1974. Now gets bullied by an app.
- **2B, The 3 A.M. Fridge Light.** Ancient, speaks only in what you almost admitted to yourself. Terrifying and gentle.
- **3C, Dez, Wi-Fi Dead Zone.** Teenage goth deity of "can you hear me now". Cuts out mid-sentence at the worst moments.
- **4A, The Last Blockbuster Aisle.** Elderly, dignified, dying slowly, still insists on late fees.
- **Basement, Something Without a Name.** Pays rent in cash, exactly on time. Nobody has seen it. This is the season arc.

## Pilot: "Noise Complaint"
**Cold open:** Dee's first night. The ceiling is moaning. She storms upstairs with a broom and finds Dez mid-existential crisis, buffering at a wall. Dee: "I do not care what you are, you are on a lease."

**A story:** Gary is being evicted by a parking app. Dee, who hates him, runs a one-woman guerrilla campaign to illegally park a Honda Civic on the roof of City Hall.

**B story:** The Fridge Light tells Dee the one thing she has been avoiding. She spends the episode doing everything but listening.

**C story:** Dez keeps cutting out right when she is supposed to say she is scared. Four seconds of black. It lands.

**Tag:** A cash envelope under Dee's door, one month of rent, from the basement. The building has been paying for itself for sixty years. Someone wanted her to stay.

## Season arc
The basement entity is the first thing humans ever feared: the dark in the corner of a room. It is not paying rent out of malice. It is trying to keep the building so nobody turns the lights off for good. Finale: Dee decides whether the world needs its small gods, or whether she just needs somewhere to live.

## Why it works
One building, six rooms, cheap to animate. Every episode is one eviction notice, one small god, one very human problem. Gods are endlessly swappable: God of the Group Chat Left on Read, God of the Stair That Isn't There.
